## 1. What, when, and on which charts

**Instruments he trades** [D19, D29]:
- S&P 500 (SPX, traded as a CFD at his broker)
- GBP/USD ("GU")
- GBP/JPY ("GJ")
- Gold (XAU/USD)

He added them one at a time over 5 years. GJ came first, and was the only pair he traded when he turned profitable [D29].
- **Beginner rule:** trade one instrument only and delete the rest of your watchlist. Add a second only after you are profitable on the first [D29, D46]. Early in the course he allows "one or two pairs" for study, and no more [D4].
- In a slump he switched from the S&P to forex because S&P price action was bad [D41]. That is a tweak he makes as a profitable trader, not a beginner rule.

**Session and time window:**
- His main window is the New York Stock Exchange open, 9:30 ET. He takes a trade **within the first ~1.5 hours** of NYSE open. If nothing sets up in that window, there is no trade. An open trade can run past it [D29].
- The open is the highest-probability time. New money comes in, liquidity gets swept and orders get filled. After that, volume dies down and the setups get worse [D25].
- No trades before the open. Pre-market moves into a level are noted but not traded; on D18 he skipped a pre-market reaction and only entered after the open [D18].
- He trades forex pairs at the NY open too [D35, D49, D53].
- London open is fine for people whose timezone suits it. Avoid the Asian session [D29, D46].
- Pre-market S&P and the S&P during the London session are "not trading" [D22, D34].
- **Exception:** on Day 54 he looked for an Asian-session GJ trade because CPI hit GBP during London. He didn't take it.
- Trading late in the session is a known mistake, because volume is low [D45, D47]. On D52 he closed a trade at about 2 pm ET partly because it was "too late in the market."

**Timeframes:**
- Monthly and weekly: context.
- Daily: **the bias**.
- 4H and 1H: confirmation and the main areas to trade from.
- 15m, 5m and 1m: execution. Forex is usually executed on 15m and 5m. The S&P is usually executed on 5m and 1m [D35].
- Every concept works on every timeframe [D10, D12, D20, D28].
- **"Higher time frames hold higher power."** A break of structure on a lower timeframe against the higher-timeframe trend is usually just a retracement on the higher timeframe [D4, D6, D18, D36, D44, D47]. He first teaches this on D4: if the 4H is bearish and the 15m breaks up, wait for the 15m to turn back down and trade with the 4H.
- A **line chart** on the higher timeframes is a quick way to see direction without candle noise [D4, D34].

## 2. The building blocks, as TJR uses them

There are five: **liquidity sweep, break of structure (BOS), order block (OB), fair value gap (FVG), equilibrium (EQ)** [D30]. He says they are all he uses. Drop indicators, trend lines, EMAs and chart patterns [D1, D2, D30, D45].

**Candles** [D2]:
- Make decisions only on **closed** candles. That is why a BOS needs a candle close.
- Wicks show where price was rejected. A long wick with a small body shows momentum fading or indecision. A full-bodied close shows strength.
- Only single-candle reads matter. Multi-candle patterns are rejected.
- Never trade off a candle pattern alone. It is only one confluence. He reads a doji at a level as indecision while he waits for the BOS [D18].

**Trend** [D4]:
- Uptrend = higher highs and higher lows. Downtrend = lower highs and lower lows. The third state is **consolidation**: no progress either way, so no trend and nothing to trade [D4, D6].
- Markets move on momentum; each push in the trend direction tends to go past the previous extreme [D4].
- **Trade with the trend.** Look for lower-timeframe setups in the direction of the higher-timeframe trend [D4].

**Swing highs and lows** [D6]:
- A **high** is an up candle followed by a down candle. Its price is the **highest wick of those two candles**.
- A **low** is a down candle followed by an up candle. Its price is the **lowest wick of the two**.
- It only takes two candles. You can't lump several bumps together into one "high".
- Only the **most recent** high and low matter [D6].

**Liquidity** [D8, D10, D12]:
- Resting orders: stop orders that take people out of trades, and pending orders that put people in [D8].
- Above highs: breakout buy stops plus the stops of anyone short. Below lows: breakout sell stops plus the stops of anyone long [D10].
- Institutions need opposite-side volume to fill their orders, so they push price through those pools, then move it the other way [D8, D10].
- Mark highs and lows, "most importantly **prominent** highs and lows" [D12]: e.g. the high that started a big trend, a double top, a high that caused a daily BOS. Judged by eye; no formula. Later days add equal highs/lows [D22] and the Asian, London and prior NY session highs/lows [D47, D52–54].
- Not every high or low gets used, which is why he waits for confirmation [D12].
- Liquidity is a **magnet** and gives **targets**: a short targets the prior prominent lows [D12]. Building blocks are also "price magnets" [D32, D37].

**Liquidity sweep** [D6, D10, D12, D30]:
- Price trades **beyond** a high or low, taking the resting orders. A wick through a swing without a body close is the classic sweep (a "fake-out" beginners mistake for a BOS) [D6]. It can be tiny: a high that "barely gets wicked" still counts [D12].
- **A sweep alone is not an entry.** The confirmation is a **BOS in the opposite direction** afterwards. No reaction, no trade: "hands off" [D8, D12, D30].
- **Settled:** the sweep candle's shape doesn't matter. Price can wick or close past the level (D35 agrees). The BOS that follows is what validates it.

**Break of structure (BOS)** [D6]:
- His checklist:
  1. Identify the trend.
  2. In an **uptrend**, watch the **most recent low**. In a **downtrend**, watch the **most recent high**.
  3. BOS down = a candle **closes below** the most recent low. BOS up = a candle **closes above** the most recent high. The body must close beyond the swing's wick.
- **A wick through is never a BOS**, however far it goes; it's usually a sweep. A still-forming candle doesn't count [D2, D6].
- In D6 terms a BOS is a **trend flip** [D6]. It confirms orders were filled and structure shifted [D8, D30].
- In execution he "monitors the highs" after a sweep [D22]; "form a 15m high, then close above it" [D53]. That matches D6: after a low is swept, the lower timeframe is in a mini-downtrend, so you watch its most recent high.
- A lower-timeframe BOS against the higher-timeframe trend is a higher-timeframe retracement, not a reversal [D6, D18].

**Order block (OB)** [D10, D20, D22, D24]:
- The **whole leg** that caused the liquidity sweep, right before the BOS. First mentioned on D10. Big orders were filled there, so price tends to come back into it.
- Treat it as one OB per trend change **on each timeframe** [D20, D22, D24].
- **Drawing it:** beginners box the whole leg. He boxes "from the wick… down to the start of the body" of the leg's candle [D24]. **AMBIGUOUS:** which candle on multi-candle legs.
- His **first-choice retracement entry** [D20]. He enters on touch [D26] or drops a timeframe and waits for a BOS inside it [D22, D24].
- An OB can also be a take-profit level [D24].

**Fair value gap (FVG)** [D14, D16, D18]:
- A price range with **no opposing orders**, left by a big candle. Price tends to come back into it [D14].
- **3-candle pattern** [D16], candle 2 is the big candle:
  - **Bullish FVG:** from the **top of candle 1's wick** to the **bottom of candle 3's wick**.
  - **Bearish FVG:** from the **bottom of candle 1's wick** to the **top of candle 3's wick**.
  - Overlapping wicks of candles 1 and 3 = **no FVG**.
- **With the trend only** [D16]: a retracement and continuation tool, never a reversal tool, after a BOS sets the direction.
- **Don't enter on the touch** [D16, D18]. Wait for a candle closing in the trade direction, or a lower-timeframe BOS.
- **Invalid** once price pushes past the whole gap [D18] ("disrespected" [D22, D24]).
- They are everywhere and not all work [D16]. Mark several and see which one price reacts to; trim a gap where a higher-timeframe wick already covers it [D18].
- His use: mainly finding where a retracement goes, a **"patient entry"** when sweep + BOS gives bad RR, or extra confirmation [D14, D18].
- An FVG's start, 50% and end can all be take-profit levels [D18].

**Equilibrium (EQ)** [D26, D28]:
- The 50% level of the current swing (swing low to new high in an uptrend; swing high to new low in a downtrend).
- Shorts belong in the upper half of a range and longs in the lower half.
- Tool: Gann box or Fib with only 0, 0.5 and 1 [D28].
- Draw it as soon as a retracement starts [D33]; re-anchor if a new extreme comes first [D28].
- **Never enters on EQ alone.** "Is it even in a discount" is his bare minimum [D36]; then he wants an OB, FVG or BOS inside it.
- He sometimes calls the upper half a "discount" for shorts [D26, D36]; he means "the better price."
- **EQ-only plays** [D48]: wait for a candle to **close back beyond the EQ line** together with a BOS.

**Ranking of retracement tools** [D20]: order block, then FVG, then equilibrium.

## 3. Daily bias: the top-down method (Days 34–36, used daily in Days 43–54)

1. **Weekly:** which way is it going? A line chart is fine. Note weekly building blocks [D34].
2. **Daily = the bias:** the direction of the last daily BOS [D34, D35]. "We trade daily moves, not weekly ones."
   - If the daily disagrees with the weekly, **follow the daily**; treat it as a weekly retracement heading for a weekly FVG, EQ or OB [D36, D48, D53].
3. **4H and 1H:** ideally agree with the daily; "at least" daily and 4H should agree [D49].
   - A 4H retracing against the daily can be the setup forming. Mark daily and 4H blocks in the daily direction, plus EQ, and wait [D34, D36].
   - If daily, 4H and 1H all conflict and price is choppy, **skip the instrument today** [D44, D48, D52].
4. **Mark the POIs** on 1H and 4H in the bias direction: OBs, FVGs, EQ and liquidity pools [D49].
5. **At the session,** wait for price to reach a POI, drop to 15m/5m/1m and wait for sweep → BOS → OB/FVG/EQ in the bias direction. No reaction at a POI = "it was never there" [D36].
6. **Never force a bias** from fundamentals or opinion [D35].
7. **When the daily breaks the other way, switch** [D41]. The only acceptable losses are higher-timeframe reversals, not execution errors [D49].

His framing [D34]: decide where you'd enter on the daily/4H/1H, then use the lower timeframes for a "sniper" entry aiming at higher-timeframe targets.

## 4. Entry models (Days 30, 32, 33, 41)

All four begin with a sweep and then a BOS in the bias direction:

| # | Model | Entry | Risk level |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sweep + BOS | Close of the BOS candle | **Riskiest** |
| 2 | Sweep + BOS + OB retrace | When price comes back into the OB | Middle |
| 3 | Sweep + BOS + FVG retrace | When price fills the FVG and reacts | Middle |
| 4 | Sweep + BOS + (OB or FVG) + past EQ | As 2 or 3, also past the 50% line | **Safest** |

- If model 1 gives a bad risk-to-reward ratio (RR), wait for the retrace [D26, D30].
- **Day 41 change:** after losses he **stopped taking model 1**; only models 2–4. Treat this as his current rule.
- **Beginner filter** [D29]: only setups with the daily bias and the overall trend.
- **Worked example** [D22]: daily BOS → price returns to daily OB → 1H BOS inside it → NY open → 15m OB and 15m BOS, entry on that close → targets 1H liquidity, FVG middle, daily highs (~1:12 RR).
- **Worked example, FVG version** [D18, S&P]: a 15m BOS down leaves one clean 15m FVG (others had overlapping wicks) → pre-market retrace ignored → at the open price pushes into a 5m FVG → 1m BOS, short on its close → TP1 liquidity below and TP2 a small OB both hit; rest stopped at break-even.
- **Earliest recipe** [D12]: "sweep of liquidity, a break of structure, an [FVG] reacted… that's where I'm entering." D16 homework: after a bearish BOS, enter when price fills the FVG and closes bearish, stop above the previous high, target the previous lows.

**Session "forex strategy"** (only partly described): the "London fakeout" on GJ, selling after London takes out a high with a 15-pip stop [D1, D7]; 30-minute boxes at session opens [D45, D54]; Asian/London/prior NY highs and lows as liquidity swept before the real move [D53]. **The box rules are never taught in Days 1–55.**

## 5. Stop loss (Day 38)

- **Default:** beyond the liquidity-sweep extreme, even when entering at an OB or FVG. "The stop is the invalidation point."
- Not just past the nearest minor high inside the move.
- **Add a spread buffer** (~0.5 points or your broker's spread).
- **Exception** (he dislikes it): if RR is terrible, stop past the OB or FVG.
- **Match the stop to the setup's timeframe** [D22].
- Examples: ~4 S&P points below a 5m sweep low [D38]; 15-pip GU stop above the Asian high [D52].

## 6. Targets and trade management (Days 12, 18, 21, 36, 37, 53)

- Take profits at building blocks: liquidity, FVG start/50%/end, OB edges, EQ levels. A short targets the **prior prominent lows** [D12, D18].
- **TP1** = the first draw on liquidity on **one timeframe higher** than execution (5m entry → 15m liquidity). Aim for **at least ~1:1 RR to TP1** [D37].
- Usually 3–4 TPs [D37]. Keep targets short when the 4H is against you [D34].
- **At TP1: take 50% off and move the stop to break-even** [D53, D21]. No multi-day swings [D36].
- **Don't close early on emotion** [D21]. (On D52 he closed manually on a stalled late trade and admitted he shouldn't have taken it.)
- Exits when the bias is invalidated mid-trade: **AMBIGUOUS.**

## 7. Risk and position sizing (Days 13, 19, 21, 27, 29, 39, 46)

- **Pre-set your daily risk** before the session and write it down [D29].
- **Risk 1% per trade.** 1–3% is acceptable; more than 3% is "doing something wrong" [D27, D46].
- **D13 version: 1–3% per day in total**, split as one trade at 1–3%, two at 1.5% or three at 1%. No setup = 0% risk that day. Treat a small account like a big one [D13].
- **One trade a day** is the default. Win or lose, you're done [D21, D29]. If two, they share one day's risk. Never trade to win back a loss [D41, D49, D51].
- **Sizing:** a lot-size calculator per trade, or a "set lot size" where your typical minimum stop equals 1% [D39]. Check contract size per lot for indices.
- **Risk tiers** [D39]: normal 1×; de-risk 0.5× for news-heavy weeks and bank holidays; confident 2× (beginners never).
- **Path to real money** [D27]: demo until 3 months in a row of >10% at 1% risk, one trade a day → tiny live account → funded account after 3 profitable live months → payouts into live.
- Think in percentages and win rate. Judge results monthly and yearly [D21, D51]. Expect to lose money for the first year or two; treat it as tuition [D13, D17].

## 8. News rules (Day 19, applied later)

- **Check Forex Factory every morning.** Keep red/orange and grey (bank holiday) items for your currencies: USD for S&P and gold, GBP and JPY for the GBP pairs.
- **Never trade CPI, PPI, FOMC or NFP days** [D19, D43, D47, D48, D50, D54].
- **Fed Chair speaking:** no trade that day or the day after [D25, D33].
- **US bank holidays:** no trading, forex included [D38, D39].
- **Other red/orange news:** don't trade before it; wait 15–30 minutes after; if it caused big wicks, no trade today; otherwise trade defensively at reduced risk [D19]. Write your own spike threshold into your plan (his example: a 5-point spike within a 5m candle) [D29]. News at 10:00 ET means no trade until ~10:30 [D19, D49].
- **A news-packed week** is low probability: de-risk or sit out [D19, D38].
- Reading direction: good for the base currency pushes the pair up, good for the quote currency pushes it down; good for USD is bad for S&P, gold, GU. **But price action beats news direction** [D19, D54].
- News before the open often means the move is over by the session [D47].

## 9. When NOT to trade

- Any news situation in section 8.
- The setup is against the daily bias [D29, D44].
- Daily, 4H and 1H conflict, or price is choppy with no direction [D44, D48, D49, D52].
- Price never reached your marked building blocks [D48].
- You've already traded today or used today's risk [D29].
- It's past your time window [D29, D45, D52].
- "Look for a reason **not** to trade" [D19]. "The best traders take the most days off" [D44]. Make the market leave you "no other option" [D44].

## 10. Psychology and discipline

- Treat a win like a loss: no emotion, study why it worked [D21].
- Balance fear and overconfidence [D25, D27].
- Don't change strategy after losses; find the repeating mistake and make a small tweak [D41].
- It's always your fault, never the market's [D1, D11, D41].
- One strategy, your own bias, screen time over secret ingredients [D45]. Expect a year or more [D45].
- After a loss: journal it and finish for the day. No revenge trading, over-trading or raising risk [D11, D51].
- **Patience** [D17]: a written step-by-step plan; if the steps haven't all happened, no trade. Come in **not wanting to trade**. Waiting one or two more candles costs little. The dopamine comes from clicking the button.
- **Root causes** [D15]: write your three biggest discipline problems and find the emotion behind each (greed, fear, overconfidence, confusion). E.g. over-trading for money → get a job; closing early from fear → risk less or go back to demo.
- **Habits and goals** [D3, D5, D7]: one uncomfortable daily habit; three long-term goals with 10 short-term steps, read aloud daily. Don't copy anyone or compare yourself with friends.
- **Rest:** at least one full day off the charts each week [D9].
- **Meditation:** 30 minutes alone in a dark room, focusing on breathing [D17]; journal your thoughts and distractions and cut screen time [D51].
- Trading should feel boring. "You are the algo" [D11, D27, D29].

## 13. Mechanical version: IF/THEN rules to code

Defaults come from the transcripts. **PARAM** items are free choices TJR doesn't specify. Times are America/New_York.

### Inputs
- OHLC for 1m, 5m, 15m, 1H, 4H, D, W (and M) for SPX, GBPUSD, GBPJPY, XAUUSD.
- Economic calendar (impact, currency, time, event name). Exchange holidays.
- Account balance, contract size per lot, spread per instrument.
- **PARAM** `swing_mode`: `"two_candle"` (TJR's rule, D6; default) or `"fractal"` with `swing_len` (not his). **PARAM** `eq_tol` (equal-highs tolerance).

### A. Definitions
1. **Swing high/low** (D6, `two_candle`):
   - **Swing high:** bar `i` is up (`close > open`) and bar `i+1` is down (`close < open`). Level = `max(high[i], high[i+1])`, confirmed when bar `i+1` closes.
   - **Swing low:** bar `i` down, bar `i+1` up. Level = `min(low[i], low[i+1])`.
   - Track only the **most recent** confirmed swing high and low.
   - **PARAM** `doji_policy` (`close == open`): default, continues the previous candle's direction. **ASSUMPTION.**
2. **BOS** on timeframe T (D6), a state machine:
   - IF `Trend(T) == up` AND a T candle **closes** below the most recent swing low THEN BOS down, `Trend(T) = down`.
   - IF `Trend(T) == down` AND a T candle **closes** above the most recent swing high THEN BOS up, `Trend(T) = up`.
   - A wick beyond without a close is **never** a BOS. Only closed candles count (D2).
   - Seed `Trend(T)` from the first close beyond either swing.
3. **Trend(T)** = direction of the last BOS on T.
   - **PARAM** `consolidation_filter`: IF the last N swings make neither HH+HL nor LH+LL THEN `Trend(T) = range`, no bias from that timeframe (D4, D6). **ASSUMPTION** for N.
4. **Liquidity levels** (D10, D12):
   - **"Prominent"** swings, **PARAM** `prominence`. Default keeps any of: swings confirmed on the next higher timeframe; swings that were the level of a BOS; swings that start a trend leg.
   - Equal highs/lows within `eq_tol` (D22).
   - Asian, London and prior NY session highs/lows (D47, D52–54).
   - Prior day high/low. **ASSUMPTION.**
5. **Sweep of a high:** a candle's high trades **above** a liquidity high, by any amount, close or not (D6, D12, D35). Lows mirror.
   - **Valid** only if an opposite-direction BOS follows on the execution timeframe within `PARAM k` bars (D8, D12, D30).
   - `sweep_requires_close_back` defaults to **False**.
6. **Order block:** after a sweep and a BOS, the leg that made the swept extreme (bars from the last opposing swing to the sweep extreme).
   - **PARAM** `ob_mode`: `"leg"` (full range, beginner) or `"wick_to_body"` (extreme wick to that candle's open, TJR's).
7. **FVG** (D16), formed on bar `i` (candle 3):
   - **Bullish:** `low[i] > high[i-2]`, zone `[high[i-2], low[i]]`.
   - **Bearish:** `high[i] < low[i-2]`, zone `[high[i], low[i-2]]`.
   - Overlapping wicks = no FVG.
   - Keep only FVGs **in the direction of `Trend(T)`**, created after the BOS (D16).
   - **Invalidated** once price pushes past the far edge (D18). **PARAM** `fvg_invalidate_on` = `"close"` (default) or `"wick"`.
   - **PARAM** `fvg_refine`: trim where a higher-timeframe wick already covers the zone (D18).
8. **Equilibrium:** for a bullish leg `EQ = (swing_low + swing_high)/2`; re-anchor on a new high before EQ is reached. Discount for longs = price ≤ EQ; premium for shorts = price ≥ EQ.

### B. Day filters (IF any is true THEN no trades today on that instrument)
9. Today has **CPI, PPI, FOMC or NFP** for the instrument's currencies (SPX/XAU/GU → USD; GU/GJ → GBP; GJ → JPY).
10. The Fed Chair speaks today (or spoke yesterday, per D25).
11. Today is a US bank holiday (all instruments).
12. Other red/orange news before or inside the window: no entry until `release + PARAM settle_min` (15–30); IF the release 5m candle range > `PARAM spike_threshold` THEN skip the day; ELSE `risk_tier = "derisk"`.
13. The week has ≥ `PARAM` red events for the currency → `risk_tier = "derisk"` all week.

### C. Bias (once per day, pre-market)
14. `bias = Trend(D)`.
15. IF `Trend(4H) == bias AND Trend(1H) == bias` THEN `setup_state = "aligned"`.
16. ELIF `Trend(4H) != bias` AND price is past daily EQ in the bias-favoured half AND inside a daily/4H OB or FVG in the bias direction THEN `setup_state = "htf_retrace_into_poi"` (optional switch).
17. ELSE no trade on this instrument today.
18. Weekly opposite to daily does not block a trade, but IF `Trend(W) != bias` THEN cap targets at the weekly retracement target (TP1–TP2 only).

### D. Points of interest
19. POIs = 1H and 4H OBs, FVGs, EQ zones and liquidity pools in the bias direction, built pre-market.

### E. Session window
20. Entries only 09:30–11:00 ET. A pre-market touch of a POI never triggers an entry (D18); it can arm a setup that completes after 09:30. **PARAM** `allow_london` for forex. Asian session never.
21. Max **1 trade per instrument-day** and **1 per account-day** by default. **PARAM** `max_trades = 2` with risk split so the total ≤ one day's risk.

### F. Entry (execution timeframe X = 15m/5m for forex, 5m/1m for SPX)
22. **Arm:** price trades into a POI during the window.
23. **Sweep:** while armed, X sweeps **against** the bias (takes a low for longs, a high for shorts) → record `sweep_extreme`.
24. **Confirm:** an X candle then closes beyond the most recent X swing in the bias direction (latest X swing high for longs, low for shorts) → `confirmed = True`.
    - IF no such close within `k` bars THEN drop the sweep (D8, D12).
    - Build the X OB (rule 6), any FVG from the BOS leg (rule 7), and the leg's EQ.
25. **Entry models** (`PARAM enabled_models`, default {2, 3, 4} per D41):
    - Model 1: enter at the BOS candle close.
    - Model 2: limit at the OB's near edge (or market on touch, per D26).
    - Model 3: price trades into the FVG and **reacts**: an X candle closes in the bias direction from the zone, OR (preferred, D18) a bias-direction BOS on the next lower timeframe. Never on the touch alone.
    - Model 4: Model 2 or 3 AND entry on the bias-favoured side of EQ.
    - EQ-only variant (D48): a candle closes back beyond EQ plus a fresh X BOS.
26. **RR check:** IF `(TP1 − entry)/(entry − stop) < PARAM min_rr_tp1` (default 1.0) THEN skip model 1 and wait for 2/3/4.
27. **Cancel** the setup if price closes beyond `sweep_extreme` before entry, or the window ends.

### G. Stop
28. `stop = sweep_extreme ∓ buffer`, `buffer = max(spread, PARAM pts)` (~0.5 SPX points).
29. **PARAM** `alt_stop`: IF RR < threshold THEN stop past the OB/FVG edge instead.

### H. Position size
30. `risk_$ = balance × 1% × tier` (1.0 normal, 0.5 derisk, 2.0 confident — default off).
    - Hard cap: total risk opened in a day ≤ 3% of balance (D13).
    - `size = risk_$ / (|entry − stop| × value_per_point × contract_size)`, rounded down to the lot step. Or a fixed "set lot size".

### I. Targets and management
31. **TP1** = nearest opposing liquidity or building-block edge on the next higher timeframe than X.
32. **TP2..TP4** = the next building blocks beyond, capped per rule 18.
33. **At TP1:** close **50%** and move the stop to break-even. Split the rest across later TPs (**PARAM**).
34. Never widen the stop. No manual exit on feelings.
35. **Optional time stop** (D52): IF TP1 not hit after `PARAM` hours, or past ~14:00 ET, AND an X/15m BOS has closed against the position, THEN close.
36. After any closed trade, no new entries today.

### J. Account-level rules
37. Daily max loss = the pre-set daily risk; stop for the day when reached.
38. Log every trade with the journal fields from section 11.

### Parts that need discretion and can't be coded faithfully yet
- **Which highs and lows are "prominent" liquidity** (D12, by eye). The two-candle swing rule is exact but marks many tiny swings on low timeframes; the `prominence` filter and HTF alignment carry a lot of weight.
- **How far a sweep must go.** Any amount counts, so `k` and the prominence filter decide what actually gets traded.
- **OB boundaries** on multi-candle legs.
- **Which of several FVGs** price will respect (D18).
- **Which swing to anchor EQ to**, especially in ranges.
- **"Choppy" and "drastic news reaction"** thresholds (ATR, range compression, wick size), never given.
- **Whether a POI "reacted"**, and touch entry vs waiting for a lower-timeframe BOS.
- **The 30-minute-box session strategy**, mentioned but never taught.
- **The daily-vs-4H divergence exception** (rule 16).
- **Choosing between instruments** on the same day.
- **Exits when the higher-timeframe bias flips mid-trade**, early closes, late-session judgement.
- **Risk-tier choice** and the demo → funded → live progression.
